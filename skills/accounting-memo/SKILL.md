# Technical Accounting Memo

## Before writing
- If another accounting skill covers the topic (for example ASC 842, 606, 805, or 740), run its procedure first. The memo documents that analysis; it does not replace it.
- List anything that is missing: facts, contract terms, amounts, dates, and the reporting framework (US GAAP, private company alternatives, or IFRS). Never invent facts. Label every assumption as an assumption.

## Citation rule
- Cite the Codification as Topic-Subtopic-Section-Paragraph, for example ASC 842-10-25-2.
- Cite a paragraph number only when you are confident it is correct. Otherwise cite at the topic or subtopic level and add the tag [verify in Codification].
- Paraphrase the guidance; do not paste long passages.
- Never fabricate a citation, a firm position, or an SEC comment.

## Structure
1. **Header.** To, From, Date, Re. Use a client ID if firm policy requires it. Include the period and the engagement.
2. **Issue.** One to three precise questions, for example: "Should the lease of X be classified as a finance or an operating lease under ASC 842?"
3. **Background and facts.** Only the facts relevant to the issue, each with its source (contract section, email, schedule). Mark assumptions separately.
4. **Relevant guidance.** The criteria or requirements that apply, cited as above.
5. **Alternatives considered.** Every reasonable view, including the one rejected, and why it was rejected. Reviewers look for this section first.
6. **Analysis.**
   - Apply each criterion to the facts, one at a time.
   - Show the numbers (PV tests, percentages, allocations), cross-referenced to the workpaper.
   - Call out the judgment points explicitly and say what evidence supports the view taken.
7. **Conclusion.** A direct answer to each question in the Issue section. No hedging beyond what the facts require.
8. **Financial statement impact.** Journal entries, balance sheet and income statement effects, and cash flow classification.
9. **Disclosure considerations.** The required disclosures that this conclusion triggers.
10. **Open items and next steps.** Anything still pending: documents, confirmations, reviewer questions.

## Style
- Neutral and precise. Write in the present tense for analysis and the past tense for events.
- Define each term once and use it consistently afterward.
- Short paragraphs, with numbered criteria.
- Every number must tie to the workpaper.
- Default length is 2–4 pages, unless the issue is complex or the user asks for something longer or shorter.

## Self-review checklist (run it and report the result before delivering)
- Every fact is sourced or labeled as an assumption.
- Every criterion in the guidance is addressed. None are skipped.
- Alternatives are stated and rejected with a reason.
- The conclusion answers the Issue exactly as it is worded.
- The entries balance and the amounts tie to the analysis.
- All uncertain citations are tagged [verify in Codification].

## Delivery
Deliver the memo as a shareable document, or as a .docx file if the user asks for Word. If the user only wants a quick view, give it in the reply in the same structure.

## Review mode
When the user supplies a memo to review:
- Grade it against the structure and the checklist above.
- Flag missing alternatives, unsupported facts, logic gaps, and wrong or unverifiable citations.
- Suggest specific rewrites rather than general comments.

## Boundaries
The memo documents an analysis for the reviewer. The final accounting conclusion belongs to the engagement team or technical reviewer.
